🧸 Kazkar — Privacy Policy

Revised September 19, 2026. Applies to the Android app "Kazkar" (package com.kazkar.kazkar).

In short: Kazkar collects no personal data at all — neither the child's nor the parent's. The child's name lives only on your device. There are no ads, no analytics, no accounts, and no tracking. The only thing the app does over the network is download the books themselves.

1. Who we are

Kazkar is an app with illustrated children's stories where your child becomes the hero of the tale. It is built by an independent developer (Ukraine). You can write to us at the address in the Contact section — it is the only way to reach us, and we answer any privacy question.

2. What data we collect

None. We do not collect, store on servers, share with third parties, or sell any information about you or your child. The app has no accounts, no registration and no place where anything is "submitted": the only input fields are the optional child's name and age and the answer to the parental challenge, and all of them stay on the device.

The app also has no analytics, no advertising SDKs and no crash reporting — that is, none of the tools data is usually collected with.

3. What stays on your device

Everything below lives inside the app's own storage and never leaves it:

  • The child's name (optional) and age (optional) — used only to place the name inside the story text. You can skip them entirely: the story is then read with the book's default hero name.
  • Settings: light or dark theme, app language, read-aloud speed, and the "use the child's name" toggle.
  • Downloaded books — texts, illustrations and audio, kept for offline reading.

Backup of the app's data is disabled at the app level (allowBackup=false, plus explicit data-transfer rules). This means the child's profile and settings do not enter Google's cloud backup; transferring the data to a new device is turned off by the app's rules as well.

4. Network: why the app uses the internet

The only network use is downloading books (texts, illustrations, audio) from our public static catalog on GitHub Pages. These are ordinary anonymous HTTPS read requests: they carry no child's name, no age, no device identifiers and no cookies. We add nothing of yours to the request — only the file's address and the standard technical headers any internet connection adds (the app engine version, supported compression formats), which identify neither the child nor the device.

As with any internet connection, the hosting provider (GitHub) technically sees the request's IP address — otherwise it could not deliver the file. Any web server sees the same when you open a page in a browser. We have no access to those logs and do not link them to any person; GitHub describes its own handling in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

5. Read-aloud: how the stories sound

Prepared narration

The stories are narrated in advance — ordinary audio files pre-rendered with a speech-synthesis voice (not a microphone recording) and stored together with the book. When such a file plays, nothing happens beyond playing a recording from your device: the synthesis happened before the book ever reached it. The narration reads the story with the book's default hero name, not with your child's name.

The app does not use the microphone — it does not even hold that permission. No voice of a child or a parent is ever recorded or sent anywhere.

System speech synthesis

If a page has no recording, the "Listen" button hands that page's text to your device's system speech engine (Android text-to-speech — for example "Google Speech Services" or your manufacturer's voice). That is a separate program on the device itself, not our server: we have no servers. If personalization is on, that text contains the child's name.

Here is a nuance worth stating honestly: your system text-to-speech settings may have a network voice selected. Such a voice synthesizes speech on its provider's servers (Google's, for instance), and the page text is then processed there — under that provider's policy, not ours. We do not make that choice, we cannot see it, and we have no influence over it.

If you would rather avoid this, any one of the following is enough:

  • install an offline voice in your system text-to-speech settings (synthesis then happens on the device). When a voice is missing, the app itself offers a shortcut to those settings — behind the parental gate;
  • turn personalization off in the parent zone — the text then carries the book's default hero name instead of the child's name;
  • listen to the prepared narration — no synthesis happens on your device while it plays.

6. The games meadow

The mini-games collect nothing and download nothing either. The only exception is the narration: if a phrase has no prepared recording, its text is read by the same system speech engine as in section 5 (game phrases never contain the child's name). More than that, the games store nothing even on the device: the star counter and the state of a game live only while the game is open and reset as soon as the child leaves it. No scores, no statistics, no history of play exists.

The games have no timers, no losing, no leaderboards and no comparison with other children — there is nothing to compare, because no data ever leaves a game.

7. Errors and diagnostics

If something goes wrong in the app, the error is written only to the device's own local log (Android logcat) — and disappears with it. No crash reports are sent anywhere: the app contains no crash-reporting service. In the release build the page text is deliberately kept out of that log entry, so that the child's name cannot end up there even by accident.

8. Ads, analytics, purchases

  • There are no ads and never will be — this is a children's app.
  • There is no analytics or tracking — no analytics or advertising SDKs.
  • The app does not request or use the Advertising ID.
  • There are no accounts and no registration.
  • The current version has no purchases. If they appear (book unlocks for parents), they will sit behind a parental gate and this policy will be updated in advance.

9. Children's privacy

The app is directed to children and follows the Google Play Families policy, as well as COPPA and GDPR-K requirements. We collect no personal data from children — neither directly nor through third-party libraries: the app contains no such library.

The app shows children no ads, has no chat, messaging or content sharing between users, and does not lead a child to third-party sites. Settings, profile reset and the single external link (to this page) are hidden behind a parental gate — a simple arithmetic challenge a child does not pass on their own. The gate collects and stores nothing.

10. How we keep data safe

The strongest protection is having no data at all, and that is how the app is built. On top of that:

  • the profile and settings live in the app's private storage, inaccessible to other apps;
  • cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are disabled by the app's rules;
  • everything downloaded travels over HTTPS only and only from our own catalog; the app cannot be redirected to a foreign host;
  • the app contains no third-party analytics, advertising or crash-reporting SDK — there is none of them to hand anything to. The only exception is the device's own system speech engine (section 5).

11. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

We store nothing and have nothing to delete on servers — nothing reaches them. Everything that exists is on your device, and you are in full control:

  • Reset the profile in the parent zone — removes the child's name and age from the device;
  • Clear the app's data in Android settings — removes everything: profile, settings and downloaded books;
  • Uninstall the app — after that nothing remains on the device.

Data is kept exactly as long as you keep it: until you delete it in one of these ways. You do not need to send us a deletion request, but if you have a question, write to us — the address is in the Contact section.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes (for example, when purchases appear), the updated version will be published on this page with a new revision date. The page address never changes, so the links inside the app and in Google Play always lead to the current version.
